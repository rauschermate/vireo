## Post

Every AI tool I use writes markdown all day — Claude, Cursor, ChatGPT. Plans,
READMEs, notes, summaries. It's quietly become the language everything speaks.

But when I went to actually *read* all that markdown, every option felt wrong.
Half the apps showed me the raw syntax — `##`, `**`, `- [ ]` — like it's 2011.
The other half wrapped a plain text file in a 300MB app with "vaults" and plugin
marketplaces and a monthly subscription, just to display it.

Markdown is a small miracle: it's just text, it outlives every app that touches it,
and your notes, your READMEs, your agent, and your future self all speak it. It
deserved better than being treated like plumbing.

So I built Vireo — a native Mac app that shows your markdown as a clean document and
hides the syntax *while you edit*. Bold looks bold. Headings look like headings. And
the file on disk stays byte-for-byte plain `.md`, yours forever. It's Swift on Apple
Silicon, about 4MB, opens instantly. No vaults, no plugins, no subscription.

It's free, and it's out today. I built it because I wanted it to exist — I'd love if
it's useful to you too.
