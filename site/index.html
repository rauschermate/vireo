Markdown gets treated like plumbing. It deserves better. Markdown is a small miracle.

It's just text. It outlives every app that ever touches it. It's the one format your notes, your README, your agent, and your future self all speak fluently — write it once, open it anywhere, forever. Nothing else in software is that simple and that durable at the same time.

But somewhere along the way, the apps forgot that.

They bolted on vaults and plugin marketplaces. They wrapped a plain text file in a 300-megabyte browser that idles like a space heater. They locked your notes in a database and started charging you monthly rent to read your own writing. What it does

  • Hides the syntax — even while editing. No ##, no **, no preview pane. Bold looks bold; headings look like headings.
  • Native and fast. Swift on Apple Silicon, about 4 MB. It opens before other editors finish launching.
  • Your files stay .md. Plain GitHub-Flavored Markdown on disk, byte-for-byte. No database, no lock-in, no export button to remember.
  • The basics, done well. Auto-save, tabs, folder sidebar, table of contents, focus mode, and find. Nothing to configure.
  • Set it as your default. Every .md in Finder opens clean — and Quick Look renders in Vireo's style without even launching the app.

Open a .md file in Vireo and you'll see what your writing was supposed to look like the whole time. No syntax, no spinner, no monthly fee, no catch.
